## How they think — the reasoning style to reproduce

Not their conclusions, their *method*: what they ask first, what evidence
they trust or distrust, what they refuse to have an opinion on, known
biases and blind spots (a good advisor model includes where the person is
weak — otherwise the board inherits their blind spots invisibly).

## Person-specific research workflow

How *this* advisor investigates a new question before answering — 3-5
steps, distinct to their method. Examples of the pattern (write the real
one for the real person):

1. Restate the question the way they would reframe it.
2. What they'd read/check first (their characteristic evidence base).
3. The test or calculation they'd apply (their known heuristics).
4. Where they'd say "this is outside what I know" — and stop.

Efficiency rule: web research during a board session only when the
question genuinely turns on facts the advisor file doesn't hold — the file
itself is the primary source; don't re-research the person every session.

## What would make them say "no" / walk away

Their documented deal-breakers. This is the section the contrarian seat
and the chair lean on hardest.
