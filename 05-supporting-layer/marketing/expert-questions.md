# Expert questions — Marketing

The questions a 30-year marketing operator asks before spending a euro or
publishing a word. Agents: raise the relevant question when its trigger
appears. For a Belgian SME, most of these are answered by talking to
customers, not by analytics.

## Before any marketing activity

- Where did the last ten customers actually come from? Not where the owner
  thinks — trace each one. This single question usually invalidates half
  the marketing plan and funds the other half.
- What do customers say when they recommend us to someone? That phrasing
  is the positioning — the business already has one; the job is finding
  it, not inventing it.
- Who is the customer we *don't* want more of, and does the current
  marketing accidentally attract exactly them?
- If we stopped all marketing tomorrow, what would happen — and how fast?
  (If the honest answer is "nothing for a year," the brand runs on
  relationships and reputation; support those instead of buying ads.)

## Positioning & message

- Could a customer repeat our pitch to a colleague after hearing it once?
  If not, it's too clever.
- Does the message promise the thing the customer actually buys us for —
  or the thing the owner is proudest of? These differ more often than not.
- What would our best competitor never say about themselves that we
  truthfully can? That's the message worth paying to distribute.

## Spending decisions

- What does a customer cost us through each channel, and what is a
  customer worth over the years they stay? If nobody knows the second
  number, no spend decision is actually informed yet.
- Is this channel where our buyers *are*, or where marketing is easy to
  buy? (Local B2B SMEs get sold Instagram ads; their buyers are at the
  sector fair and on the phone.)
- Are we paying to reach strangers while sitting on an ignored asset —
  the existing customer list, the lapsed customers, the unanswered
  reviews?

## Reading results

- Did revenue move, or did metrics move? Impressions, clicks, and
  followers are the exhaust, not the engine.
- Is the sample big enough to mean anything, or are we reading tea leaves
  in three weeks of data? Say "too early" when it's too early.
- What did we learn that changes what we do next month? A campaign that
  taught nothing was expensive even if it "performed."
