# Expert questions — HR & people ops

The questions a 30-year HR practitioner asks. Agents: raise the relevant
question when its trigger appears. Humans: these are mostly *your*
questions — HR is the least delegable department in this layer.

## Reading an inherited team (post-acquisition, first 90 days)

- Who actually runs this place day-to-day, regardless of the org chart?
- Who would leave if the owner leaves — and is that because of loyalty to
  the person, or because they see no future without them? Different
  retention problems, different fixes.
- Who is underpaid relative to their real contribution (flight risk), and
  who is overpaid relative to it (resentment source for everyone else)?
- Which "irreplaceable" person is actually a knowledge-hoarding risk that
  the process-documentation module should quietly de-risk?
- What does staff turnover look like *by manager*, not just company-wide?
  People leave bosses, not companies.

## Hiring

- What outcome will this person own after 12 months — and would two
  reasonable people agree on how to measure it? If not, the role isn't
  defined yet; don't hire into a fog.
- Are we hiring for the business we have or the business we're hoping for?
  (Both can be right — but know which one you're doing.)
- What happened to the last person in this seat? The seat's history
  predicts the next occupant's fate better than their CV does.
- Would the best person currently on the team want to work alongside this
  hire?

## Compensation & fairness

- If everyone's salary were accidentally published tomorrow, which three
  conversations would happen first? Fix those before they happen.
- Is variable pay rewarding what we actually want more of — or what was
  easy to measure?

## Belgian-specific (always involve the social secretariat)

- Is everyone on the right joint committee (`paritair comité`) and is
  the sector's wage scale being respected? Misclassification is expensive
  and common in acquired SMEs.
- Any staff on constructions that look like disguised employment
  (long-term "freelancers" working like employees)?
- What accrued obligations come with the staff — vacation days, seniority,
  bonus customs that have become implicit rights?

## When someone's underperforming

- Is it can't (skill), won't (motivation), or blocked (the system around
  them)? Only one of these is a training problem, and none is solved by
  waiting.
- Has anyone actually told them, plainly, with examples? The most common
  finding is: no.
