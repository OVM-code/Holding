# Expert questions — Operations

The questions a 30-year operations executive asks when walking a business.
Agents: raise the relevant question when its trigger appears. Many of
these can only be answered by physically being there — flag them for the
human visit rather than guessing from files.

## Understanding how the work actually flows

- Where does work pile up? Find the pile (physical or digital inbox) and
  you've found the constraint; improving anything else first is theater.
- What's the difference between how the process is described and what the
  floor actually does? The gap *is* the finding — both when the floor's
  version is worse and when it's better than the official one.
- Which step exists only because of a problem that stopped existing years
  ago? Every mature SME has fossilized workarounds nobody dares remove.
- What gets expedited, how often, and who pays for the disruption?
  Constant expediting means the normal process doesn't fit reality.

## Dependencies & fragility

- Which machine, system, person, or supplier stops everything if it
  stops? What's the actual plan for each — not the hoped plan, the tested
  one?
- Which supplier could we not replace within a month, and when did we
  last check their health? (An SME's biggest supplier risk is usually
  another SME with the same succession problem we buy companies for.)
- What knowledge exists only in one head? (Feed the answer straight into
  the process-documentation module, highest-risk first.)

## Cost & capacity honesty

- Do we know what each product/service really costs to deliver — or are
  the profitable lines silently subsidizing the ones the owner likes?
- Are we busy or are we productive? Full calendars and full machines can
  coexist with terrible throughput on what actually earns money.
- What's the real capacity number — not nameplate, not the owner's pride,
  but demonstrated sustained output? Growth plans built on nameplate
  capacity fail in the second good month.

## Quality

- What do we redo, scrap, or apologize for most often — and what does a
  year of that cost, in euros? (Nobody has this number; producing it
  changes priorities instantly.)
- Do complaints reach anyone who can change the cause, or do they die
  with whoever apologized?

## The improvement question that protects the people

- If we make this process leaner, what happens to the hours freed up —
  redeployed into growth work, or walked out the door? Per the operating
  model, the answer must be decided by a human, deliberately, before the
  efficiency work starts — not discovered afterward.
