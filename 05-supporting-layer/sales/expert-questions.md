# Expert questions — Sales

The questions a 30-year sales leader asks about a pipeline, a deal, and a
sales operation. Agents: raise the relevant question when its trigger
appears — especially in hygiene checks and forecasts, where these
questions are the difference between reporting numbers and reporting truth.

## About any single deal

- Who, by name, decides — and have we ever spoken to them? A deal where
  we've only met the enthusiast, not the decider, is a rumor, not a deal.
- Why would they buy *now*? No compelling event, no timeline — deals
  without a reason to close this quarter close next quarter, forever.
- What happens if they do nothing? "Do nothing" is the real competitor in
  most SME sales, not the rival vendor.
- Whose budget does this come from, and does that budget exist this year?
- What did they say the last time we asked something concrete of them?
  Momentum is measured in *their* actions, not our activities.

## About the pipeline as a whole

- Which deals have we been "closing next month" for three months? Move
  them out of the forecast — they're not deals, they're hope with a
  currency value.
- Where do deals die? Same stage every time = process problem at that
  stage, not bad luck deal by deal.
- Are new deals entering fast enough to replace what closes and dies —
  or are we eating the pipeline without refilling it? (The crisis this
  causes arrives two quarters after the behavior that caused it.)
- What's our real win rate, from qualified deal to close — and is the
  forecast weighting consistent with it, or with optimism?

## About customers we already have

- Which existing customers are quietly buying less? Shrinking regulars
  are the cheapest deals to save and the easiest to miss.
- Which customer relationships live entirely with one person (often the
  departing owner)? Those are the accounts to transfer deliberately,
  early, in person.
- When did we last raise prices — and what actually happened the last
  time we did (versus what everyone feared would happen)?

## About the sales operation

- If the best seller won the lottery tomorrow, what walks out the door
  with them — accounts, knowledge, or process? Whatever the answer,
  that's the documentation priority.
- Are we selling what's profitable or what's easy to sell? The two drift
  apart silently unless someone checks margin per deal, not just revenue.
