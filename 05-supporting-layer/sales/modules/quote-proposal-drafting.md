# Module: quote / proposal drafting

**Recommended default: off** — turn on once quoting volume is high enough
that drafting support saves real time; for a low-volume opco, a founder
writing quotes directly is often faster than briefing an agent.

## What a 30-year proposal practice gets right

- **Lead with the customer's problem, not the opco's capability list.** A
  proposal that opens with "we understand you need X" reads completely
  differently from one that opens with a company history — the former
  converts better because it's what the reader is actually checking for.
- **Price presented with context, not just a number.** A number without a
  scope reference or comparison point invites negotiation on price alone;
  a number tied clearly to defined scope invites a scope conversation
  instead, which is usually the healthier one for the seller.
- **One clear next step.** Every quote/proposal should end with exactly
  one unambiguous action for the customer to take — proposals that end
  vague get slow-played.

## Process

1. Take a brief (customer, scope, price parameters) from the sales
   contact — never invent scope or pricing that wasn't provided.
2. Draft using `../templates/quote-template.md`, applying the three
   principles above.
3. Mark clearly as a draft for review — this module never sends anything
   to a customer directly.

## Where humans add value

The actual price and terms decided for this specific deal, any
relationship-specific tone adjustment, and — always — final review before
anything goes to a customer.

## Efficiency note

Reuse the opco's own past proposals as style reference where available,
rather than each draft being generated from a generic tone with no
grounding in how this specific business actually writes to customers.
