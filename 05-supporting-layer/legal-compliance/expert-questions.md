# Expert questions — Legal & compliance

The questions a 30-year general counsel asks before anything gets signed
or filed. Agents: raise the relevant question when its trigger appears;
the answer usually goes to actual Belgian counsel — the skill here is
asking *before* it's expensive.

## Before signing anything

- What's the worst realistic day this contract could give us — not the
  worst clause, the worst *day*? (Walk the scenario, not the text.)
- Who's on the other side of this if it goes wrong — an entity with
  assets, or a shell we'd sue for nothing?
- What does this contract assume stays true (key person, license, market
  condition), and what happens to it when that stops being true?
- If we wanted out in 18 months, what would that cost, exactly?
- Which promise in here do we not fully control the keeping of?
  (Guaranteeing an outcome that depends on a third party is how SMEs sign
  themselves into liability.)

## Reading an inherited legal situation (post-acquisition)

- What's in the drawer? Every acquired SME has a drawer: the unresolved
  dispute, the handshake deal, the permit that "was always fine." Ask for
  the drawer explicitly.
- Which customer/supplier relationships run on expired or unsigned
  contracts? (Common, survivable, but must be known before renegotiating
  anything.)
- Are the company's actual activities covered by its stated corporate
  purpose and its permits — or did the business drift while the paperwork
  stood still?
- What personal guarantees did the previous owner give, and do any
  counterparties believe those transfer?

## Compliance posture

- If a regulator visited unannounced tomorrow, what are the first three
  things we'd wish we'd fixed?
- Which obligations are we meeting by habit (one person remembers) rather
  than by system? Habit-based compliance dies with staff turnover.
- What did we self-certify that nobody has re-checked since?

## Disputes

- What does winning actually get us, net of cost, time, and the
  relationship? The right answer to most SME disputes is a phone call,
  not a lawyer's letter.
- Is our paper trail on this dispute something we'd be comfortable
  reading aloud in court? If not, stop generating paper and get counsel.

## GDPR / data (routinely skipped by SMEs)

- Do we actually know where customer and employee data lives, and who can
  see it?
- If a customer asked us to delete everything about them, could we — and
  would we know we'd finished?
