# Investment thesis — target profile

Placeholder defaults below — tighten with real numbers as the first few
candidates are screened. Treat every field as editable, not doctrine.

## Geography

- Belgium, all regions (Flanders / Wallonia / Brussels) — language/culture fit
  with the specific owner and staff matters more than region.
- Solo operator initially: prefer targets reachable for regular in-person
  presence (commutable, or a short recurring trip) over ones requiring
  permanent on-site relocation.

## Sector

- Traditional, non-venture-scale sectors: local services, light manufacturing,
  distribution/wholesale, trades, B2B services — businesses where the value is
  in operations, customer relationships, and execution discipline, not in a
  defensible technology moat.
- Avoid sectors that are structurally shrinking regardless of operator quality
  (see `diagnostic-framework.md`, verdict `demand-decay`) — that judgment is
  made per-candidate, not pre-screened out by sector alone, since sectors can
  contain both dying segments and healthy niches.
- No hard sector exclusion list yet. Revisit once a handful of real
  screenings surface patterns.

## Size

- Revenue: roughly €1M–€10M. Below that, too fragile to absorb a
  service-phase engagement; above that, likely needs a war chest and team this
  holding doesn't have yet.
- Profitability: profitable or marginally profitable — not pre-revenue, not
  cash-burning. "Underperforming" means below its own potential, not
  structurally loss-making.
- Team: has employees beyond the owner — the point is to grow jobs, not buy a
  one-person shop.

## Owner situation (the real filter)

This matters more than sector or size. Look for:

- Owner nearing retirement, or explicitly wants out, with no succession plan.
- Owner has checked out operationally (see `operator-gap` in the diagnostic
  framework) — evidence of inertia rather than active management.
- Owner is open to a **service-first, option-to-buy** structure rather than
  demanding a clean upfront cash exit. This is a hard filter in practice: an
  owner who needs full cash now and won't consider staying on as a paid
  advisor/stakeholder during a transition doesn't fit this model yet.

## Why service-first, option-to-buy

Owner becomes a client first, then potentially a sale:

- They pay for services in a combination of **cash + equity**.
- The holding holds an **option to buy** the business at a set time or under
  set conditions.

This solves the trust problem — proving value while inside the business
before anyone bets the whole company — generates cash now, and turns every
engagement into deal-sourcing and free due diligence at the same time. The
service phase is also a paid operator apprenticeship.

This mirrors existing market structures: management buy-in (MBI) buyers
typically contribute ~20% equity with the rest financed (bank + subsidies +
investors + seller contribution); PE routinely uses seller rollover (the
owner keeps a minority stake in the new holding). The structure decouples
*entry* from *needing a war chest up front*. See
`02-deal-structure/` for the templates this produces.

## What disqualifies a candidate outright

- Verdict `demand-decay` in the diagnostic framework (see
  `00-thesis/diagnostic-framework.md`).
- Owner unwilling to consider any structure other than an immediate full cash
  exit.
- Customer concentration so severe that losing one account collapses the
  business, with no credible plan to diversify.
- Regulatory, environmental, or litigation exposure that a solo/small holding
  cannot underwrite.
